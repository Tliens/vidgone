Remove a Logo from a Video — Corner Bugs, Channel Marks, Station IDs

The classic watermark problem: a small logo parked in the corner of every frame. Here's the clean way to deal with it.

Corner logos are the most common watermark: a channel bug, a stock-footage mark, a broadcaster's station ID. Because the logo never moves, one mask fixes every frame — which makes this the case VidGone handles best. And because nothing is uploaded, even sensitive or unreleased footage stays on your machine.

Match the removal method to the background

Quick fix (fast diffusion) is ideal when a logo sits on sky, walls or water. Smart repair (directional inpainting) is the best balance for most logos on textured scenes. Fine repair rebuilds texture at multiple scales — slower, and the strongest on complex backgrounds. If the logo sits on top of important moving detail, Soft blur is the honest fallback.

How to do it in VidGone (3 steps, nothing uploaded)

  1. Open VidGone and drop your video in. The file is read straight from your disk by your own browser — there is no upload step, because there is no server to upload to.
  2. Mark the watermark. Scrub the timeline to the scene with the mark, then brush over it or draw a box. The same mask applies to the whole clip, which is exactly what a fixed-position logo needs.
  3. Export a clean MP4. Every frame is repaired and re-encoded with hardware acceleration (WebCodecs). AAC audio is copied losslessly when the source uses it. Preview the result before downloading.

Tips that visibly improve the result

Cover slightly beyond the logo's edge so the engine anchors on clean pixels; check 3–4 scenes across the timeline before exporting; and prefer the source resolution — the repair runs at full resolution either way, so there's nothing to gain from downscaling first.
